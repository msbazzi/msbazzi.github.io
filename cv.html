Research interests

Computational mechanobiology of cardiovascular adaptation, integrating subject-specific fluid–solid interaction, constrained-mixture growth and remodeling, multimodal imaging, and data-driven constitutive modeling.

Research experience

Postdoctoral Scholar — Stanford University

Cardiovascular Biomechanics Computation Laboratory, Palo Alto, CA · February 2024–Present

  • Lead computational modeling efforts to understand and predict tissue-engineered vascular graft (TEVG) remodeling by integrating longitudinal imaging, scaffold mechanics, fluid–structure interaction, and constrained-mixture growth and remodeling.
  • Develop physics-constrained neural-network approaches to discover interpretable constitutive laws for vascular tissues, with applications to venous mechanics.
  • Lead experimental–computational studies of developmental changes in inferior vena cava geometry, hemodynamics, and mechanics in a juvenile large-animal model.
  • Develop computational functionality within the open-source SimVascular framework for cardiovascular fluid–solid–growth simulations.

Graduate Research Assistant — University of Minnesota–Twin Cities

Department of Biomedical Engineering, Minneapolis, MN · September 2018–January 2024

  • Developed subject-specific computational models to investigate how altered blood rheology in sickle cell disease affects systemic hemodynamics and oxygen transport in the cerebral vasculature.
  • Built image-based fluid–structure interaction models of ascending thoracic aortic aneurysms to identify biomechanical markers associated with disease progression and outcomes.
  • Developed multiscale growth-and-remodeling models linking vascular wall mechanics to longitudinal changes in aneurysm geometry and arterial adaptation.
  • Integrated medical imaging, experimental measurements, finite-element modeling, and transport simulations to study cardiovascular disease across multiple spatial and temporal scales.

Research Scientist — PUC-Rio

Laboratory of Microhydrodynamics and Flow in Porous Media, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil · January–September 2018

  • Developed mathematical and computational models of two-dimensional multiphase flow in porous media for reservoir-flow and pressure analyses.
  • Implemented numerical simulations to investigate fluid compressibility and reservoir response under varying pressure conditions.
  • Applied finite-element and numerical methods to characterize transport processes in complex porous-media systems.

Graduate Research Assistant — Pontifical Catholic University of Rio de Janeiro

Laboratory of Microhydrodynamics and Flow in Porous Media, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil · January 2016–January 2018

  • Developed mathematical models of viscoelastic liquid-sheet and liquid-curtain rupture to determine how non-Newtonian stresses regulate interfacial stability.
  • Derived long-wave models for planar and axisymmetric geometries and implemented numerical methods to solve the resulting governing equations.
  • Performed linear stability analyses demonstrating how viscoelastic normal stresses can delay or suppress liquid-sheet rupture.
